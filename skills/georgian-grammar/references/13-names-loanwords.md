# 13 · Names, places, nationalities, brands and abbreviations

Load this for people's names (Georgian and foreign), countries and nationalities, Latin-script brands, and abbreviations.

Sources: A §2.1.2.18, §2.2.11.3 · B §2.1.14.1.5–6 · C "სადაურობა / წარმომავლობა" · Ar (via GZ) §4, §10 · LI naming and transliteration cautions.

---

## NAME-01 · First name + surname [norm]

- Only the surname declines. A consonant-stem first name before a surname is a **bare stem** (no `-ი`):
  - `გალაკტიონ ტაბიძე`, `ნოდარ დუმბაძე`, `მერაბ კოსტავა`, `ჯონ სმიტი`;
  - `ნოდარ დუმბაძემ`, `ნოდარ დუმბაძის`.
  - ✗ `ნოდარი დუმბაძე`, ✗ `ნოდარმა დუმბაძემ`.
- Vowel-stem first names are unchanged: `ილია ჭავჭავაძე`, `აკაკი წერეთელი`.
- Full declension rules for names, including the vocative: CASE-08. Titles before names: CASE-09.
- Initials: `ი. ჭავჭავაძე`.

## NAME-02 · Foreign names and places [norm]

- Spell them by pronunciation, not by their original spelling: `სემ ალტმანი`, `დემის ჰასაბისი`. European *h* → `ჰ` (SPELL-08).
- **Consonant-final** names and places take `-ი` in the nominative and decline normally: `ლონდონი`, `ლონდონში`, `ლონდონის`; `ბერლინი`, `ჯონი`, `ჯონმა`.
- **Vowel-final** foreign names keep their vowel (CASE-08): `ტოლსტოის`, `დელიში`.
- Compound foreign place names take a hyphen: `ნიუ-იორკი`, `ლოს-ანჯელესი`, `სან-ფრანცისკო`.
- Use the established Georgian name of a country, not a new transliteration (NAME-04).

## NAME-03 · Latin-script brands and product names [convention]

- In tech, business and news text, established brand and product names often stay in Latin script: `Google`, `iPhone`, `Microsoft`, `OpenAI`.
- Georgian endings attach after a hyphen. Choose the ending by how the final sound is pronounced:
  - `Google-ის` (gen.), `Google-მა` (erg.), `Microsoft-ში`;
  - `OpenAI-მ` (ends in a vowel sound), `iPhone-ით`.
- ✗ `Googleის`, ✗ `Microsoftმა` (no hyphen).
- Either write a given name in Latin, or use its established Georgian spelling. Don't mix both for the same brand in one text.

## NAME-04 · Countries and nationalities [norm]

People take `-ელი` or their own word; the adjective for things and languages takes `-ური`/`-ული` (ADJ-05, SPELL-02).

## NAME-05 · Abbreviations [convention]

- **Read letter by letter:** the ending follows a hyphen: `აშშ-ში`, `აშშ-ის`, `სსრკ-ის`.
- **Read as a word:** decline it like a noun, with no hyphen: `ნატოს`, `გაეროს`, `ნატოში`.
- Choose one style and keep it within a text.
